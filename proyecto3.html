How do you design a typeface that speaks the language of a generation without losing its bond with the place that inspires it?

Bitty Type Font

Client
Biblioteca Municipal de Chinchón
Industry
Cultural institutions
Location
Chinchón, Madrid, Spain
Year
2025
Tools

Typeface design

Bitty is a bitmap typeface designed pixel by pixel, created for the municipal library of Chinchón. The brief called for connecting with a younger audience and conveying a contemporary character to refresh the institution's image ahead of the 2025 Book Fair. The response was a typeface built like video games and early computer screens — evoking a certain digital nostalgia for some, and speaking the visual language of a generation for others.

Using Fontstruct as the design tool and taking inspiration from the geometry of the town's towers and local architecture as the base module for each character, it became a way of anchoring the typeface to its territory without sacrificing modernity.

What I underestimated was the process itself. Designing a typeface — even a bitmap one — involves a level of detail and decision-making I hadn't fully anticipated. Every glyph presents its own challenges: spacing, consistency, rhythm across characters, how a curve behaves at this scale, whether a particular form reads clearly or starts to break down. Most of those challenges resolved themselves naturally as the work progressed, but they required patience and a kind of autonomous problem-solving that taught me more than I expected.

Bitty is designed for headlines. In large sizes it holds its character — playful, nostalgic, territorial. As point size decreases, legibility starts to suffer, and that's an intentional trade-off: this is more about a display typeface.
