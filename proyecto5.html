What remains of human creativity when machines also create?

Yorokobu #147

Client
Yorokobu
Industry
Editorial, publishing

Tools

Editorial design

This project responds to a real brief from Yorokobu magazine: design the cover for issue #147, summer edition, and make it work simultaneously as an editorial piece.

The proposal starts with a question that feels very present in 2026 — what remains of human creativity when machines also create? The answer doesn't come from the future. It comes from the back cover of a school notebook: that free space no one evaluated, where you drew purely for the pleasure of it.

The cover portrays Yorokobu's name written by hand with primary colour pencils, surrounded by doodles, stickers, and small winks that form a constellation of meaning. Most of it celebrates creativity — and only in one very discreet turn does it generate the small tension of a topic that divides the present. The concept connects with Yorokobu's own origins: a magazine that bet on paper and ideas when the entire world was saying the opposite, and that has been proving ever since that creativity as a philosophy doesn't expire.

The cover works on three layers of experience. The first is immediate — colour and energy with a certain disruptive character at first glance. The second is emotional — the nostalgia that appears when you recognise the notebook, it shouts childhood, it speaks analogue. The third is conceptual, and it happens when you start reading the elements and understand that there's a dialogue between the human and the artificial, between what we celebrate and what we defend.

The imperfection of the line is intentional. It's not an error — it's a signature, and it's the personality that defines this proposal. Exactly what no algorithm can replicate. A manifesto without banners. A small declaration of love for everything genuine, spontaneous, and deliberately human.
