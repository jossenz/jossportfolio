1. Opening

How might we help young people in Madrid explore who they are — before deciding what they want to be?

Year

Tools

Método MAD is a vocational guidance app designed for the Ayuntamiento de Madrid. MAD stands for Mapea, Aprende, Decide — and doubles as a nod to Madrid's airport code. It helps teenagers aged 16–20 discover their strengths, values, and possible paths through games, not forms.

The project covered the full UX process: desk research with real data sources, two user personas, empathy mapping, competitive analysis, problem definition, user flows, and a mid-fidelity prototype from the first statistic to the final screen.

5. Problem definition

The real problem wasn't lack of information. It was lack of self-knowledge.

How might we help teenagers aged 16–20 in Madrid explore their identity, strengths and values progressively and without pressure — so they can make more conscious decisions about their future, whether that's university, entrepreneurship or autonomous learning?

Existing tools treated vocational guidance as a one-time test. Método MAD treats it as an ongoing process — something that grows with the user over months, not minutes. We also identified a key insight: vocational guidance can be a form of mental-health prevention, not a response to a crisis.

6. The solution

An app that guides you without telling you what to do

Método MAD works through daily micro-games, a progressive vocational profile and three equal paths — Formación, Emprende, Aprende — none treated as better than the others. The more you use it, the more it knows about you. Distribution matters too: the app is reached through the Ayuntamiento's youth portal, where young madrileños already look for guidance.

7. Design decisions

Three decisions that shaped everything

◐

Color and typography

Lavender as primary works in both light and dark mode — modern without being aggressive. Clash Display for headings: editorial personality without feeling childish. General Sans for body: readable on small screens, balances the display type.

↻

Navigation and flow

Daily streaks and micro-games reduce the pressure of big decisions — borrowed from Duolingo. Three equal paths because we wanted no route to feel like the "correct" one. University is valid. So is everything else.

◎

Visual voice and tone

The app talks to you, not at you. Questions read like conversations, and the choice cards signal a safe, low-stakes space. Inspired by Headspace's emotional warmth and Observatorio Gen Z's editorial tone.

8. Reflections

What I learned and what I'd do differently

This project taught me that the best UX decisions come from the emotional context, not just the functional one. If I were to continue, I'd add a social layer — connecting with people going through the same process was one of the key unmet needs. I'd also run usability tests with real teenagers to validate the game mechanics before assuming they work. And I'd push the gamification even further: adding illustrated, friendlier elements that match the kind of user we designed for — bringing warmth, dialling down the educational tone, and getting closer to the goal of being a genuinely useful tool for new generations.
